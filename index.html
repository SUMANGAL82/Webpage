Where the research lives

/ 08 focus areas
⛓ Dependence Modeling

Copulas & Multivariate Statistical Models

Modeling complex dependence structures using copula-based approaches, with applications to correlated multivariate responses and flexible joint distributions.

ϟ Inflated Response Models

Inliers, Early Failures & Count Data

Developing statistical models for multivariate count and lifetime data exhibiting multiple inflated observations, inliers, and early-failure phenomena.

∫ Survival & Reliability

Survival Analysis & Reliability Theory

Statistical modeling of lifetime and censored data, cure-rate models, aging intensity functions, and reliability measures for understanding failure and survival mechanisms.

σ Bayesian Statistics

Bayesian Inference & Computation

Bayesian estimation and uncertainty quantification using computational methods, including Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC), Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC), and related simulation-based techniques.

∑ Nonparametric Statistics

Nonparametric Inference & U-Statistics

Developing distribution-free and nonparametric inferential procedures using U-statistics, resampling methods, lower partial moments, and jackknife empirical likelihood.

Δ Statistical Machine Learning

Machine Learning & Predictive Modeling

Exploring statistical and machine learning methods for estimation, prediction, and modeling complex relationships in data.

↻ Time Series & Forecasting

Statistical Learning & Time Series Analysis

Studying temporal dependence, stochastic processes, statistical learning methods, and forecasting techniques for time-dependent data.

◇ Causal & Latent Variables

Causal Inference & Latent Structures

Exploring causal relationships, unobserved heterogeneity, and latent variable models for statistical inference in complex observational data.

Currently

My current research explores nonparametric inference using U-statistics, lower partial moments, and reliability measures — including a median-based aging intensity function.

I am also interested in censored survival data, cure-rate models, latent variable modeling, causal inference, statistical learning, and time-series analysis. On the computational side, I work primarily with R, RStan, and Python, employing maximum likelihood estimation, Bayesian computation, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, and other simulation-based techniques for statistical modeling and inference.

I have a deep passion for teaching and genuinely enjoy spending time in the classroom. I love discussing mathematical ideas, interacting with students, and exploring different ways of understanding a concept. I believe that a classroom is not simply a place to deliver lectures, but a space for curiosity, questions, meaningful discussions, and shared learning.

I enjoy exploring new ideas, learning from others, and engaging in academic discussions and collaborations that broaden my understanding of mathematics, statistics, and their applications.
