Teaching & Academic Experience

A Classroom Is Where Questions Become Ideas.

Teaching is one of the most enjoyable parts of my academic life. I love being in the classroom, discussing mathematical ideas, working through problems, and exploring different ways to understand a concept. Some of the most rewarding moments come when a simple question leads to an interesting discussion or when a difficult idea suddenly begins to make sense.

My teaching experience spans mathematics, statistics, and computational methods, including calculus, probability, statistical inference, optimization, and numerical analysis. Through my faculty appointments and teaching-assistant roles, I have had opportunities to interact with students from different academic backgrounds and learn from their questions and perspectives.

My approach to teaching

Understand the idea, not just the formula.

I believe mathematics and statistics become more interesting when we understand the reasoning behind the results rather than simply remembering formulas. In my classes, I try to build concepts from simple ideas, encourage questions, and connect mathematical theory with examples and applications.

I particularly enjoy the discussions that arise when students approach the same problem in different ways. Such moments remind me that learning is not always a one-way process.

My aim is to make the classroom a welcoming space where students feel comfortable asking questions, making mistakes, thinking independently, and gradually developing confidence in their understanding.
